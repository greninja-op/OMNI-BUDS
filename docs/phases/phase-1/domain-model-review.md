# Phase 1 — Domain Model Review

**Document class:** Phase 1 deliverable of the Domain Model workstream (execution prompt §4, Agent 3).
**Method.** Each domain area required by prompt sections 9–25 is reviewed against what is actually on disk in `core/src/main/kotlin/com/omnibuds/core/`: the types created, the invariants enforced **by construction**, how absence of information is represented, and the specific place a later phase would be tempted to weaken it. Section 18 records the open gaps.
**Standing rule the whole model implements.** Absence of information is `UNKNOWN` — never `false`, never `0`, never `UNSUPPORTED` (master §53; `docs/phases/phase-0/testing-governance.md` terminology block). Where a type could not express that honestly, the honest move was to not create the type; §14 is the case where that was the decision.

---

## 1. §9.1 DeviceIdentity — `device/DeviceIdentity.kt`

**Types.** `DeviceIdentity(manufacturer, model, displayName, modelId)`, all `String?` defaulting to null; derived `knownFieldCount`, `isFullyKnown`, `isEntirelyUnknown`; `mergedWith`; factories `unknown()` and `of(…)`.
**Invariant by construction.** `of()` normalises blank/whitespace text to null and trims what it keeps; `isFullyKnown` requires all four fields; `mergedWith` fills only still-unknown fields and restates nothing — two conflicting known values are a human/protocol-record conflict, not something the type may splice into a hybrid string. The primary constructor deliberately stores exactly what it is given, so no domain type silently rewrites platform evidence.
**Unknown.** `null`, and `"  "` normalised to `null`. Deliberately absent: the Bluetooth address (SEC-ID-001/003/004), and every version field.
**Weakening temptation.** Adding `bluetoothAddress` because "Android gives us one and it is convenient for lookups", or defaulting `manufacturer` to a guess inferred from a display name. The address-free rule is load-bearing: `DeviceFingerprint.identityKey()` and `SavedDeviceRecord` both inherit their honesty from it. A related temptation is to make merge *overwrite* a known field on a later read; `mergeNeverRestatesAKnownFieldWithADifferentValue` pins the opposite.

## 2. §10 DeviceFingerprint — `device/DeviceFingerprint.kt`, `ManufacturerDataEntry.kt`, `FirmwareInfo.kt`

**Types.** `DeviceFingerprint(manufacturerData, serviceUuids, characteristicUuids, deviceClass, transportCandidates, protocolCandidates, firmware)` plus `identityKey()` and `isEntirelyUnobserved`; `ManufacturerDataEntry(companyId, dataHex)` as opaque evidence; `FirmwareInfo(firmwareVersion, hardwareRevision, protocolVersion, verification)` with **no default** for `verification`.
**Invariant by construction.** Empty collections mean "nothing was seen", not "nothing exists". `TransportKind.UNKNOWN` inside `transportCandidates` contributes nothing to the key. `identityKey()` sorts every token, uppercases service/protocol text, folds the canonical address separator `':'` out of each token, escapes its own delimiters, and returns the literal `NOTHING_KNOWN_KEY` when nothing was observed. Firmware evidence is excluded from the key by design. `FirmwareInfo.verification` has no default because a type cannot honestly guess a caller's evidence tier.
**Unknown.** null per field; absent evidence excluded from the key rather than represented by a placeholder; a placeholder `ManufacturerDataEntry` reports `isEntirelyUnobserved` and is filtered out.
**Weakening temptation.** Three specific ones: (a) putting the MAC address into `identityKey()` "for exactness" — the key is a coarse bucketing device by decision, and comparisons that need exactness compare the fields; (b) folding `firmware` into the key, which makes every OTA update look like a new device and silently invalidates cached capability evidence; (c) giving `FirmwareInfo` a default `verification` so call sites stop complaining — that is the laundered-tier bug the missing default exists to prevent.

## 3. §11 DeviceSession — `device/DeviceSession.kt`

**Types.** `DeviceSession(sessionId, identity, fingerprint, classification, createdAtEpochMillis)` with `withEvidence`, `save()`, `forget()`, `isSaved` and the `temporary(…)` factory. **Connection state is deliberately absent**: no `connectionState`, no `lastStateUpdateEpochMillis`, no `isOperational`, no transition method. `DeviceState.connection` in `com.omnibuds.core.session` is the only place that fact lives (ADR-P1-020, §14), and `DeviceState.lastUpdatedEpochMillis` already reports the freshness the session used to duplicate.
**Invariant by construction.** It is a value: every update produces a new session, and the caller owns the result. `withEvidence` can only fill gaps in the identity (`DeviceIdentity.mergedWith` never restates a known field) and *replaces* a fingerprint rather than merging two discovery passes, because two passes are not one observation. `save()` changes classification only — no evidence, no state, no timestamp. `forget()` returns the value to `TEMPORARY` and explicitly does not unpair, does not clear the OS bonding record, does not delete the stored record, and asserts nothing about hardware. All seven of those properties are pinned by `DeviceSessionTest` (`TEST-P1-021`), including `savingChangesNothingExceptTheClassification` and `aNewFingerprintReplacesTheOldOneRatherThanMergingTwoObservations`.
**Unknown.** `fingerprint` is nullable (no discovery pass has run) and `createdAtEpochMillis` is `Long?`, with `anUnrecordedCreationTimeStaysUnrecorded` refusing the tempting `0`. A null time means "nothing was recorded", never "the epoch".
**Weakening temptation.** Re-adding a `connectionState` field "because the session screen already has the session", which is precisely how the phase acquired two owners of one fact before ADR-P1-020 removed it; inheriting a timestamp because "unknown looks stale in the UI"; letting `forget()` also delete the saved record (it must not claim an OS-level unpair it cannot perform); and making `save()` persist as a side effect, which is `DeviceRepository`'s rule (§16). **Not enforceable today:** `DeviceSession` still has no `init` block, so a blank `sessionId` is representable — unlike every identifier-bearing type in the model. See §18, D-7.

## 4. §12 ConnectionState — `state/ConnectionState.kt`, `state/SessionClassification.kt`

**Types.** The eleven prompt states `UNKNOWN, DISCOVERED, PAIRED, CONNECTED, IDENTIFYING, CAPABILITY_DISCOVERY, READY, CONTROL_SESSION, DISCONNECTED, TEMPORARILY_UNAVAILABLE, ERROR`; `object ConnectionStateTransitions` with `allowedNext`, `canTransition`, `isOperational`; `SessionClassification { TEMPORARY, SAVED }`.
**Invariant by construction.** A per-state forward set plus two universal rules: every state may fall into `ERROR`, and a self-transition is idempotent rather than illegal. `IDENTIFYING`/`CAPABILITY_DISCOVERY` sit *after* `CONNECTED` because they are OmniBuds' own work, not the stack's. **ADR-P1-016:** the Phase 0 six-value `SessionState` that mixed connection status with saved status is superseded by two orthogonal types, so "is this merely stale?" becomes a derivation instead of a second source of truth.
**Unknown.** `UNKNOWN` is explicitly not "disconnected": it means the adapter or device state has not been read.
**Weakening temptation.** Re-adding a `CONNECTING` or `STALE` enum member to `ConnectionState` for UI convenience — the ADR already decided those are combinations. The other: a caller bypassing `canTransition` by writing `copy(connection = …)` directly on `DeviceState`, which is possible for any data class and which `attemptConnection` re-checks precisely because it does not trust its callers. That bypass is not hypothetical — `DeviceStateTest.everyStateCanFallIntoErrorAndASelfMoveIsIdempotent` uses a private `forceConnection` helper built on `copy` to reach each state before sweeping the table, which is legitimate for a fixture and is the honest reason the rule is enforced *by the method* rather than *by the type* (§18, D-10).

## 5. §13 Capability model — `state/CapabilityState.kt`, `state/VerificationLevel.kt`, `capability/FeatureCapability.kt`, `common/TransportKind.kt`

**Types.** Six rungs `UNKNOWN, UNSUPPORTED, READ_ONLY, SUPPORTED_VOLATILE, SUPPORTED_PERSISTENT, PERSISTENCE_VERIFIED` with extension predicates `isControllable`, `isEstablished`, `isReadable`; `VerificationLevel` (INFERRED/IMPLEMENTED/LAB_TESTED/HARDWARE_VERIFIED/PERSISTENCE_VERIFIED) with `atLeast`; `FeatureCapability` carrying readable/writable/transport/protocolId/requiresConnection/verification — exactly the five extra descriptors §13 asks for.
**Invariant by construction.** The `init` block ties affordances to state: `UNKNOWN` and `UNSUPPORTED` may carry neither; `READ_ONLY` must be readable and must not be writable; the three controllable rungs must be both. `SUPPORTED_PERSISTENT` requires `verification atLeast HARDWARE_VERIFIED`; `PERSISTENCE_VERIFIED` requires `verification == PERSISTENCE_VERIFIED` — a durability claim resting on lab evidence is **unrepresentable**. Blank `protocolId` is refused (null is the honest answer). `isControllable` is derived from state, never read off the boolean flags.
**Unknown.** `UNKNOWN` with no affordance; `TransportKind.UNKNOWN` means "not determined here", not "no transport"; `protocolId = null` means the governing protocol is unidentified, an open question rather than a failure.
**Weakening temptation.** The most likely one is a `FeatureCapability.supported: Boolean` convenience added for a UI binding, which re-introduces the very boolean PROTO-CAP-001 forbids. Second: relaxing the `SUPPORTED_PERSISTENT` floor because a lab-tested fixture "should" be enough. Third, and subtler: `FeatureCapability.unsupported(feature, protocolId, transport)` **hard-codes `verification = HARDWARE_VERIFIED`** — a positive-absence record can therefore be constructed asserting hardware verification that nothing proved. The guard on that rule is prose (PROTO-CAP-001), not the type; recorded as finding Q-4 in `docs/phases/phase-1/code-quality-review.md`.

## 6. §14 DeviceCapabilities — `capability/DeviceCapabilities.kt`

**Types.** An immutable container `Map<FeatureId, FeatureCapability>` with `stateOf`, `get`, `features`, `controllable`, `readable`, `unknown`, `unsupported`, `with`, `mergedWith`, `empty()`.
**Invariant by construction — the `stateOf` rule.** **A feature that is not present is `UNKNOWN`, not `UNSUPPORTED`, and not an error:** `entries[feature]?.state ?: CapabilityState.UNKNOWN`. This is the single line the capability model exists to protect, because a lookup miss is the most common event in the system — most devices do not expose most features and most sessions have not looked yet — and the shape of that answer decides whether the UI ever renders "not supported" for something nobody checked. The constructor copies the handed-in map (explicitly, via `toMutableMap()`, because `toMap()` is permitted to return the very map it was given — a defensive copy that is sometimes not a copy is worse than one plainly never made). `with` refuses to file a record about one feature under another feature's key. `mergedWith` keeps the higher evidence rung, keeps the receiver's record wholesale on a tie (deterministic, no hybrid invented from two sources), and carries through features only one side held; the ladder is spelled out in `evidenceOf` as an explicit `when` so reordering the kernel enum surfaces as a compile error rather than a silent merge-rule change.
**Unknown.** Three distinct statements stay separate: absent from the container (`stateOf` → UNKNOWN, nothing to list), present with an explicit `UNKNOWN` record (listed in `unknown`), and present with a conclusive negative (`unsupported`, populated only by positive establishment).
**Weakening temptation.** Making a merge symmetric — "last writer wins" — which would let a weak re-discovery erase proven persistence; and populating `unsupported` from an empty discovery pass. The `unsupported` set is deliberately empty for a device that has merely not been asked, and any later "no records therefore no features" convenience breaks master §53 at one line.

## 7. §15 Battery — `device/BatteryState.kt`

**Types.** Six independent nullable fields: `leftLevel`, `rightLevel`, `caseLevel` (`Int?`), `leftCharging`, `rightCharging`, `caseCharging` (`Boolean?`); `hasAnyKnownLevel`, `isAsymmetric`, `isEntirelyUnknown`, `knownFieldCount`, `unknown()`.
**Invariant by construction.** Levels are validated to `null or 0..100` in `init`, so no sentinel (`-1`, `255`, `101`) can enter as if it were a measurement. `isAsymmetric` returns false unless **both** sides are known — comparing a known level against an unknown one would invent a difference the device never reported.
**Unknown.** `null` for levels, `null` for charging flags. `false` means "the device said it is not charging"; `null` means "the device did not say"; reading the second as the first is precisely the mistake §15 forbids. And `0` is a real reading: a flat battery is preserved as `0` and is *not* treated as missing — the asymmetry the model protects runs in both directions.
**Weakening temptation.** The classic: a UI-facing `fun levelOrZero()` or `leftLevel ?: 0` at the display boundary, which is exactly where "we never read it" becomes "0%". Second: defaulting charging flags to `false` so a binding stops being nullable. Third: collapsing the three levels into one "battery" value on devices that report only the case.

## 8. §16 Codec vocabulary and §18 Audio transport — `audio/Codec.kt`, `CodecFamily.kt`, `AudioTransportKind.kt`, `CodecRegistry.kt`

**Types.** `Codec(displayName, family)` with SBC, AAC, APTX, APTX_HD, APTX_ADAPTIVE, APTX_LOSSLESS, LDAC, LC3, UNKNOWN — all nine prompt-named entries present; `CodecFamily { CLASSIC_A2DP, LE_AUDIO, UNKNOWN }`; `AudioTransportKind { CLASSIC_A2DP, HFP, LE_AUDIO, UNKNOWN }`; `object CodecRegistry` with `all`, `byName`, `familyOf`.
**Invariant by construction.** The aptX variants are four separate entries, so "aptX supported" cannot evidence aptX HD. `LC3` carries `CodecFamily.LE_AUDIO` in its own declaration, so it cannot be treated as an A2DP codec without editing the enum. `displayName` and `name` are separate: a marketing label is not an identity. `CodecRegistry.byName` case-folds and strips separators, and returns **null** for anything unrecognised — including a vendor spelling such as "LDH", including "Hi-Res Audio", including `UNKNOWN` itself, because `UNKNOWN` is the absence of a codec rather than a codec. A key already claimed by a different codec is not stolen (first claimant wins) so resolution stays deterministic.
**Unknown.** `Codec.UNKNOWN` as a value rather than a null chain; `CodecFamily.UNKNOWN`; `AudioTransportKind.UNKNOWN`. `CodecRegistry` membership is explicitly *vocabulary, not evidence*: registering LDAC yields no `AVAILABLE`/`ENABLED`/`ACTIVE` for any device (AUD-REG-006).
**Weakening temptation.** `byName` falling back to `Codec.UNKNOWN` instead of null, which is how a UI ends up showing LDAC while the stack runs AAC; and `if (label == "LDH")` string branches at call sites, which the registry's documented extension point exists to remove. `AudioTransportKind` versus `common.TransportKind` is another collapse risk: one is the media/call path, the other how a *control channel* is reached, and a device is routinely controlled over GATT while audio runs over A2DP.

## 9. §17 Codec state — `audio/CodecState.kt`, `audio/CodecCapability.kt` — **ADR-P1-005**

**Types.** `CodecState` as an ordinal ladder `UNKNOWN < UNSUPPORTED < SUPPORTED < AVAILABLE < ENABLED < NEGOTIATED < ACTIVE`, and `CodecCapability(codec, state, configurable)` with `isActive`, `isUsable`, `supportsAtLeast`, `unknown()`, `unsupported()`.
**The decision.** §17 proposed six independent booleans and invited a better design. Six booleans permit `active = true, supported = false` and 62 further combinations of nonsense. The model splits the concept in two: **a single ladder position for the evidence dimension**, and **`configurable` as a separate boolean because it is orthogonal** — a codec can be `ACTIVE` *and* configurable, or `ACTIVE` and read-only when the OS exposes only reads; no single-valued ladder can express both, and forcing `CONFIGURABLE` onto a rung would let "configurable" masquerade as evidence that audio is carrying. `UNKNOWN` and `UNSUPPORTED` sit *below* the five positive rungs because neither says anything positive, and because AUD-STATE-005 requires an unknown codec state. The prompt's "selected" is the `ENABLED` rung.
**Invariant by construction.** `supportsAtLeast` gates **both** sides on the ladder floor, so `UNKNOWN` and `UNSUPPORTED` satisfy no positive target — an unread or rejected codec cannot be talked into supporting something. `isActive` is exactly `state == ACTIVE` and never `negotiated || enabled` (AUD-QUAL-004).
**Unknown.** `CodecState.UNKNOWN`, distinct from `UNSUPPORTED`, which requires a real negative reading.
**Weakening temptation.** The ladder's whole purpose is to be flattened at the display boundary: a `fun isSupported() = state.ordinal >= SUPPORTED` plus a UI that renders the highest-ranked codec as active would re-create the six-boolean confusion with tidier names. The second temptation is legitimate-looking: adding a `CONFIGURABLE` member back to the enum to satisfy a Phase 0 table — which is why ADR-P1-005 records the cost (any Phase 0 text implying `CONFIGURABLE` is an enum member now reads as referring to the attribute). Endpoint-differentiated support (§18 gap below) is the third.

## 10. §19 Audio quality model — `audio/AudioTransportState.kt`, `ChannelMode.kt`, `QualityMode.kt`

**Types.** `AudioTransportState(transport, codec, state, sampleRateHz, bitsPerSample, bitrateKbps, channelMode, qualityMode)` — the eight fields of master §16 — with `isActive`, `isFullyObserved`, `unobserved()`; `ChannelMode { MONO, STEREO, UNKNOWN }`; `QualityMode { SOUND_QUALITY_PRIORITY, BALANCED, CONNECTION_QUALITY_PRIORITY, ADAPTIVE, UNKNOWN }`.
**Invariant by construction.** Activity is carried as a `CodecState`, not a boolean, so `ACTIVE` is the rung and nothing else. `init` accepts only ranges a real transport can report: `sampleRateHz` positive or null, `bitsPerSample` in 8..32 or null, `bitrateKbps` positive or null — a platform adapter cannot smuggle a sentinel through as a measurement. `unobserved()` is every-field-unknown, and its KDoc names the fabrication it replaces: "a default of SBC at 44.1 kHz, 16 bit, stereo is a fabrication dressed as an observation".
**Unknown.** Each field independently; `codec` is `Codec?` where both null and `Codec.UNKNOWN` mean undetermined and neither means unsupported. `QualityMode` modes are LDAC's because LDAC is the codec whose priorities a user perceives; a codec with no such parameter leaves `UNKNOWN` rather than inheriting a plausible `BALANCED`.
**Weakening temptation.** Exactly §19's warning: a headset advertising Hi-Res licensing `96000`/`24`/`328`. The mechanism is a "friendly default" in a platform adapter that fills unreported fields, and the model's defence is that every field is nullable with validation that rejects the sentinels — the adapter must pass a real reading or pass null.

## 11. §20 Feature enumeration — `common/FeatureId.kt`, `capability/CoreFeature.kt`, `CapabilityDefinition.kt`, `FeatureCategory.kt` — **ADR-P1-008**

**Types.** `@JvmInline value class FeatureId(qualifiedName)` with `namespace`, `localName`, `segments`, `isVendorExtension`, `vendorName`, factories `of(vararg)`, `ofVendor(vendor, feature)`, `parseOrNull(raw)`; `object CoreFeature` with the fifteen §14/§20 identities, `all`, `definitions`, `isCore`; `CapabilityDefinition(feature, displayName, category, supportedValueHint)`; nine `FeatureCategory` values including `VENDOR`.
**Invariant by construction.** `of()` requires at least two segments and every segment to match lower-kebab `[a-z][a-z0-9]*(-[a-z0-9]+)*`, so identifiers are validated at creation and the qualified name is a stable contract (renaming one is a breaking change needing an ADR, because Phase 22 will key protocol records on it). `parseOrNull()` — the decode path for stored and protocol-database text — enforces **the same `MIN_SEGMENTS` floor**, so a one-segment name cannot enter through the back door that `of()` bars at the front; both paths are now pinned by `FeatureIdTest` (`TEST-P1-062`). `CoreFeature`'s `init` refuses to load if identities are not distinct, if the definition catalogue and the identity catalogue describe different sets, if any core identity is vendor-namespaced, or if anything uses the reserved `vendor` root. `isCore()` answers identity only and says nothing about support.
**Generic, not brand-conditional.** Namespaces are functional (`noise-control`, `equalization`, `input`, `sensing`, `connectivity`, `power`, `audio-quality`, `firmware`), so `if (sony)` has nowhere to live — that is §20's requirement met structurally rather than by convention. `GAMING_MODE` is the proof of intent: listed as `connectivity.gaming-mode` by function, "so no particular manufacturer's wording becomes the identity".
**Unknown.** A feature with no `FeatureCapability` record in a container is `UNKNOWN`; `supportedValueHint` is documentation about shapes seen in the wild and grants no support (pinned by `aSupportedValueHintIsDocumentationAndNeverABuiltInSupportClaim`).
**Weakening temptation, and the two defects this area actually produced.** The temptation is to treat `FeatureId` as a string wrapper and add a fourth entry point that skips the checks — every bypass tried in this phase went through a second constructor-like path, not through `of()`. First defect: the kernel's `isVendorExtension` was wrong early in the phase — its first implementation compared the *whole* namespace (`"vendor.sony"`) against `"vendor"`, so it returned `false` for exactly the ids `ofVendor` produces; a workstream found it while building the vendor guard, and it was repaired inside the phase. The property on disk is now `segments.size >= 3 && segments.first() == "vendor"`, `VendorExtension.isVendorFeature` is simply `feature.isVendorExtension` with no second check ORed onto it, and the production class KDoc no longer claims a live kernel defect (commit `a5f3bf2`, code-quality finding Q-1). **One residue, honestly recorded:** `VendorExtensionTest`'s KDoc on `aVendorNamespacedIdentityIsAcceptedAndRecognised` (lines 69–72) still asserts that the kernel property "answers `false` for this id today". It is prose only — the test asserts the correct behaviour and passes — but it is the same lie in a second place, and fixing test sources is outside this review's scope (code-quality Q-1 residual). Second defect: `parseOrNull` accepted one-segment names that `of()` refuses; that asymmetry was closed by the same commit and is now tested rather than asserted in prose (code-quality finding Q-2, `FeatureIdTest`). `aTwoSegmentVendorShapedNameIsTooShallowToNameAVendor` is the case that keeps both honest: `vendor.example` parses as a legal two-segment core identity while reporting `isVendorExtension == false` and no `vendorName`, because three segments is the shortest shape that can name a vendor.

## 12. §21 Vendor extension model — `capability/VendorExtension.kt`, `VendorFeatureMetadata.kt`

**Types.** `VendorExtension(metadata, capability, payload)` with `feature`, `VENDOR_ROOT`, `isVendorFeature`, `vendorSegmentOf`; `VendorFeatureMetadata(vendor, vendorFeatureName, documentation, confidence)`.
**Invariant by construction — the guard the vendor strategy depends on.** `init` refuses a `VendorExtension` unless the capability's identity is genuinely `vendor.<vendor>.<feature>` **and** its vendor segment equals `metadata.vendor`. That blocks both directions of the easy mistake: a vendor-only behaviour cannot be registered as or consumed as a universal core feature, and a universal feature cannot be quietly redefined by one manufacturer's version of it, because the vendor's behaviour must be filed under its own namespace where it cannot overwrite `noise-control.anc`. Nothing is renamed, folded or matched on a substring — identity is checked segment by segment, so a vendor cannot smuggle a redefinition through a similar name. `VendorFeatureMetadata.vendor` is validated against the *same* segment grammar `FeatureId` accepts and in lower case, because `Sony` would never equal a real id segment and an unmatchable-but-accepted value is a quiet no-op. Blank `vendorFeatureName`/`documentation` are refused; null is the honest answer.
**Unknown.** `anAdvertisedVendorFeatureIsStillUnknownOnThisDevice` — presence in a catalogue is not support; the honest state for "this vendor advertises it, OmniBuds has not modelled it" is a `FeatureCapability` in `UNKNOWN` with an unmapped payload, recorded rather than dropped and never rendered as a control.
**Payload discipline.** `payload` is an opaque `String?` and explicitly **not** protocol data: no command bytes, no framing, no encoded packets, and nothing in Phase 1 defines or decodes it.
**Weakening temptation.** Relaxing the vendor-segment match to a `startsWith`, or accepting a core identity "for a feature that is basically standard" — both would let a manufacturer's variant silently become the universal definition, which is the anti-lowest-common-denominator rule (PROTO-VENDOR-002/003). The metadata's regex duplicate of `FeatureId.segmentPattern` is where drift will start (§18, gap D-4).

## 13. §22 Error model and §23 result model — `common/OmniBudsError.kt`, `OmniBudsErrorCategory.kt`, `RetryClass.kt`, `OperationOutcome.kt` — **ADR-P1-004, ADR-P1-006**

**Types.** `OmniBudsError(category, operationId, detail, transport, attempts)` with derived `invalidatesSession`; sixteen `OmniBudsErrorCategory` values each carrying `retryClass` and `invalidatesSession`; `RetryClass { SAFE_TO_RETRY, RETRY_AFTER_REREAD, NEVER_RETRY }`; `OperationOutcome<T> = Success | Failure | Cancelled` with `map`, `valueOrNull`, `errorOrNull`, `isSuccess`.
**Coverage of §22.** All thirteen named examples exist; §22's list also demanded three Phase 0 omitted (`READ_FAILED`, `UNKNOWN_DEVICE`, `INVALID_STATE`), and ADR-P0-012's `GATT_FAILURE`, `RFCOMM_FAILURE`, `FIRMWARE_MISMATCH` were kept rather than dropped — **ADR-P1-006 takes the union (16) and drops nothing**, so transport-shaped specificity survives.
**The §23 decision.** §23 lists seven things an outcome might express and warns against a massive abstraction. The answer is three cases, not thirteen: timeout, unsupported, disconnected, protocol mismatch and codec unavailability are `OmniBudsErrorCategory` values *inside* `Failure`, so a call site handles three shapes with the compiler's help. **`Unknown` is not an outcome at all** — it is a property of the value, so a read that could not determine a measurement returns `Success` with that field unknown. `Cancelled` is its own case because the device's state is untouched by definition, so no re-read is owed.
**Invariant by construction.** Retry policy is a property of the *data*, not a coding habit: a timed-out write is `RETRY_AFTER_REREAD`, a rejected or unknown-effect write is `NEVER_RETRY`, so a caller cannot accidentally decide a failed write is retryable (ARCH-QUAL-009). `attempts >= 1` is enforced. `detail` is documented as diagnostic text that must not carry addresses, device names or manufacturer data. **This table is now pinned by tests, not only by the enum:** `OmniBudsErrorCategoryTest` asserts the sixteen canonical names, that `invalidatesSession` is right for **all** of them (seven uncertain-state, nine change-nothing), and that `READ_FAILED` is `SAFE_TO_RETRY`, `TIMEOUT` is `RETRY_AFTER_REREAD`, and nine write-shaped or terminal categories are `NEVER_RETRY` (`TEST-P1-063`); `OperationOutcomeTest` pins that `map` reaches only `Success` and that `Cancelled` is neither success nor failure (`TEST-P1-064`). Re-classifying a write-shaped category as retryable now fails a build instead of passing review. Honest limit: five transport-shaped categories (`BLUETOOTH_DISABLED`, `DEVICE_DISCONNECTED`, `TRANSPORT_UNAVAILABLE`, `GATT_FAILURE`, `RFCOMM_FAILURE`) have no `retryClass` assertion, so those five can still be re-tiered silently (§18, D-11).
**Weakening temptation.** Adding a `NotFound`/`Unknown` outcome case — which would collapse the most important distinction in the model into the error channel — and choosing `retryClass` at a call site instead of reading it from the category.

## 14. §24 State management — `session/DeviceState.kt`

**Types.** `DeviceState(sessionId, identity, connection, capabilities, battery, audio, revision, lastUpdatedEpochMillis)` with `attemptConnection`, `withCapability`, `withCapabilities`, `withBattery`, `withAudio`, `applyIfNewer`, `invalidatedForDisconnect`, `isOperational`, `initial(sessionId, identity)`, `INITIAL_REVISION = 0`.
**Single authoritative source, now with only one source.** This is the one value that is the truth: state flows transport/protocol → `DeviceState` → application state → UI/notification/Quick Settings, never sideways. It lives in its own `session` area precisely because composing capability, device and audio in the `state` area was a cycle — one of the three violations ADR-P1-003 records. **`connection` is the field that had two owners**, and ADR-P1-020 ended that: `DeviceSession` no longer carries a `connectionState`, so there is exactly one readable, one writable place for connection state in the module, and a session record answers "which device, and did the user choose to keep it" while a state record answers "where has it got to". That split is what makes the §24 claim enforceable rather than aspirational — before it, a tile reading the session and a screen reading the state were both legal and could disagree.
**The move rule.** `attemptConnection(next, atEpochMillis)` is the only mutator of `connection` and returns `OperationOutcome<DeviceState>`: `Success` with `revision + 1` when `ConnectionStateTransitions.canTransition` allows the move, `Failure(OmniBudsError(INVALID_STATE, operationId = "device-state.attemptConnection"))` when it does not. It neither throws — which would take the caller's error model away from it — nor silently returns the receiver, which would make "the move was illegal" indistinguishable from "nothing to do". `isOperational` derives from `ConnectionStateTransitions` rather than being re-decided per surface.
**The anti-stale-write mechanism.** Every mutation returns a new value with `revision + 1`. **`applyIfNewer(candidate)` adopts the candidate only when `candidate.sessionId == sessionId && candidate.revision > revision`** — so ordering is carried by the data rather than trusted from the caller, and a discarded value is not an error, merely older: the caller can tell, because the returned revision did not move. This is the model-level answer to §30's scenario (user sets ANC = ON, disconnect occurs, stale response arrives, state wrongly becomes ON). `invalidatedForDisconnect` keeps identity and capability history while replacing live readings with `BatteryState.unknown()` and `AudioTransportState.unobserved()` — a live reading becomes unknown, not last-known.
**Tested, and what the tests prove.** `DeviceStateTest` (12 tests, `TEST-P1-060`/`TEST-P1-061`) pins: the initial value asserts nothing about connection, battery, audio or operationality; an absent feature reads `UNKNOWN` and not `UNSUPPORTED`; a legal move advances `revision` and leaves the receiver untouched; the illegal `DISCONNECTED → CONTROL_SESSION` move is refused with `INVALID_STATE` **and changes neither the connection nor the revision**; all eleven states reach `ERROR` and every self-move is legal; `isOperational` holds only for `READY` and `CONTROL_SESSION`; `applyIfNewer` rejects older, equal, and different-`sessionId` candidates and adopts a strictly newer same-session one; the four `with…` mutators each move the revision and detach from the original; a disconnect nulls the battery reading while preserving the capability map.
**Unknown.** `initial()` has empty capabilities (reads UNKNOWN for every feature), unknown battery, unobserved audio, `ConnectionState.UNKNOWN`, `lastUpdatedEpochMillis = null`. `revision >= 0` is enforced by `require`, so a negative revision cannot poison the ordering.
**Where a later phase could weaken it.** (a) Comparing `lastUpdatedEpochMillis` instead of `revision` for freshness; wall clocks jump backwards and ADR-P1-012 explicitly assigns in-process ordering to `revision`. (b) Re-introducing a second holder of any of these fields — the mistake ADR-P1-020 exists to prevent, and the reason `DeviceSession`'s KDoc now says out loud that connection state is deliberately absent from it. (c) Bypassing `attemptConnection` with `copy(connection = …)`, which the type cannot prevent and which `DeviceStateTest` itself uses as a fixture to reach every state (§18, D-10).

## 15. §25 Repository abstractions — `persistence/DeviceRepository.kt`, `CapabilityRepository.kt`, `ProtocolRecordAccess.kt`, `SavedDeviceRecord.kt`, `DiscoveredCapabilityRecord.kt`

**Types.** Three suspend-function interfaces returning structured `OperationOutcome`s, plus two record types.
**Invariant by construction (as much as an interface can).** `DeviceRepository` exists so that two product rules live in one place rather than being re-decided at every call site: saving happens only by explicit user action, and a merely-detected device is never written here — which is why `markSeen` must *refuse* a key it does not hold rather than insert it, and why `savedDevices()` is short by construction. `forget` is local and complete (SEC-ID-007) and must never imply anything was removed *from the device*. `SavedDeviceRecord` holds an address-free `identityKey`, no MAC, no raw manufacturer data and — deliberately — **no `DeviceFingerprint` field**, even though a fingerprint is exactly what discovery produces, because archiving every observation turns "the user saved this headset" into a durable handle on a person's belongings. `ProtocolRecordAccess` is **read-only with no write method and returns ids only, never `ProtocolDefinition`s** — two databases, one seam each, so the join between a user's device list and the global protocol knowledge base can never be a side effect of a cache miss (SEC-PRIV-004/005).
**Unknown.** `load`/`find`/`record` return `Success(null)` for "nothing known", never an empty-but-unsupported stand-in; `DiscoveredCapabilityRecord.verification` is the *floor* for the whole snapshot, so a cached `PERSISTENCE_VERIFIED` cannot be reported above its tier; `discoveredAtEpochMillis = null` does not mean "recent".
**Weakening temptation — named plainly.** Three: (1) an implementation that keeps a "recently seen" table behind `DeviceRepository`, which breaks ADR-P0-004 no matter what the method names say; (2) treating a cache hit as a finding — a `PERSISTENCE_VERIFIED` from an earlier session presented as verified for *different* firmware, which the record structurally cannot distinguish because `identityKey()` excludes version evidence by design; (3) merging the two repositories "to reduce boilerplate", which is the leak the split exists to prevent. `DiscoveredCapabilityRecord`'s KDoc states the correct fix (extend the record with the firmware it was established against; never overload `identityKey`).
**Scope discipline.** §25 says "do not implement full persistence"; ADR-P1-010 goes further and chooses **no storage engine and no serialization library**, recording the forward constraints instead (stable field names, explicit version field, unknown-safe decoding, no Android objects serialised, the two databases separate). The serialization decision is deferred to Phase 17/22 with real usage to justify it.

## 16. Cross-cutting: what the model refuses to be

- **No ANC/EQ/gesture semantics — ADR-P1-007.** Prompt §26 lists `setANC`, `setTransparency`, `readEQ`, `writeEQ`, `readGestures`, `writeGestures`. `EarbudProtocol` declares only `identify`, `discoverCapabilities`, `readState`; everything else is an optional interface (`FeatureReadSupport`, `FeatureWriteSupport`, `BatteryReportingSupport`, `FirmwareReportingSupport`, `AudioStateReportingSupport`) discovered by type check, and feature reads/writes are expressed generically over `FeatureId` and `ConfigurationValue`. **ANC, EQ and gesture value shapes are deliberately not modelled**, because inventing them in Phase 1 would fabricate hardware behaviour no device has been shown to have; they travel as `ConfigurationValue` until the phases that own them define them. A mandatory `setEq()` would force every vendor to implement it, and the only implementation that compiles pleasantly is the fake prompt §53 prohibits.
- **`ConfigurationValue` closed, no `Any` escape hatch** (`config/ConfigurationValue.kt`): sealed interface over Boolean/Int/String/Mode, so an exhaustive `when` is a compile-time check; `ModeValue` keeps `technicalName` apart from `displayName` because renaming a label is a copy change and renaming an identity is a protocol change. Unknown at this layer is expressed by **omitting the entry**, never by a placeholder value.
- **Diagnostics carry no conclusion** (`diagnostics/`): `DiagnosticSnapshot.stateOf` reads an absent key as `UNKNOWN`; `SnapshotBuilder` records and never fills gaps; `TRACE`/`PACKET` carry `requiresOptIn = true` so a sink cannot enable them by accident; `DiagnosticEvent` has no field that could be populated with an address or device name, and its `message` is documented as *pre-redaction* text.
- **No clock, no Android type, no `java.*`** anywhere in the domain: timestamps are `Long?` epoch millis (ADR-P1-012) and time arrives from the caller, which is what makes every test in this model deterministic and keeps the KMP boundary real rather than intended.

## 17. How §54's review questions fare against this model

Q1 Android Bluetooth behind the domain without editing it — yes: `TransportContract`/`EarbudProtocol` are unimplemented seams and identity is `DeviceIdentity`+`DeviceFingerprint`, not `BluetoothDevice`. Q2 one abstraction over RFCOMM and GATT — yes: `TransportKind` is a value on the contract and the transport interprets nothing (`TransportRequest.payload` is opaque by decision). Q3 AAC/LDAC/aptX/LC3 without a rewrite — yes, with §18's endpoint caveat. Q4 vendor features without polluting common interfaces — yes, enforced by `VendorExtension.init` and by the `FeatureId` grammar that `FeatureIdTest` now pins both sides of. Q5 temporary without saving — yes: `SessionClassification` + `markSeen` refusing to insert, with `savingChangesNothingExceptTheClassification` and `nothingButAnExplicitSaveEverEntersTheUsersList` covering both halves. Q6 persistence verified across reconnect — **the model allows it and forbids claiming it** (`SUPPORTED_PERSISTENT` vs `PERSISTENCE_VERIFIED` floors); no evidence exists yet, and no test performs a reconnect. Q7 KMP compilation — `coreMainSourcesDoNotImportJvmOnlyLibraries`, plus `:core` now having zero production dependencies (ADR-P1-021), so nothing blocks it. Q8 every surface on one state — **yes**: `DeviceState` is the sole holder of `connection` since ADR-P1-020 removed `DeviceSession.connectionState`, and `revision`/`applyIfNewer`/`attemptConnection` are pinned by `DeviceStateTest` (§14).

**Overall.** The domain model does what prompt §9 asked — "do not over-engineer fields that have no architectural purpose yet" — and its strongest property is that the dangerous states are unrepresentable rather than discouraged: an affordance contradicting its capability state, a durability claim without durability evidence, a codec active while unsupported, an unavailable transport with no recorded reason, a vendor behaviour filed under a core identity, a write command with a retry budget, and — since ADR-P1-020 — a device with two connection states. Where a shape could not be honest, Phase 1 declined to create it (§16): ANC/EQ/gesture semantics, an endpoint-differentiated codec record, a serialisation format, a partial redactor, and any implementation of a hardware-facing contract. The verification gaps that were open when this document was first written (D-1, D-2, D-3) are closed by `a5f3bf2` and its 26 added tests; what remains is duplication that can drift (D-4, D-8), surfaces a caller can lean on the wrong way (D-9, D-10), two gaps deferred by ADR because the honest alternative was a fake (D-5, D-6), one unvalidated identifier (D-7), one half-pinned table (D-11) and one join that only Phase 2 can own (D-12) — none of them a fabricated capability.
